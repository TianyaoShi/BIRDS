01 / Beyond carbon and water

What does an AI request
mean for the living world?

The infrastructure behind a response has a life beyond the screen. Electricity generation, chip manufacturing, transport, and disposal all contribute to environmental pressures.

BIRDS connects those activities to modeled ecosystem damage, then asks how much impact is associated with a completed request and the quality of its answer.

A completed request

A defined task, model, and serving configuration.

A physical footprint

Electricity use and the lifecycle of serving hardware.

An ecological impact

Climate, water, pollution, and other ecosystem pathways.

For communities and the people reporting on AI

Carbon and water are parts of a larger picture. The results help explain why energy supply and computing choices matter. They describe modeled lifecycle impacts, not measured species losses or a site-specific assessment of a proposed data center. Impacts can occur along global supply chains as well as near electricity production.

Measurement scope and figure provenance

Results are pinned to arXiv:2605.27480v3 and exported from the preserved EMNLP figure inputs. Comparisons use the archived model selections, principally H100 with A100 supplements where recorded. Operational modeling uses US grid conditions; reported impact includes hardware lifecycle contributions.

The archived figure inputs use incremental GPU energy above the idle baseline, with datacenter overhead included in the modeling. This page preserves those processed values; it does not recompute impacts or release environmental intensity data. The paper describes the broader profiling protocol. Source filenames and checksums are recorded with the exported dataset.

Species·year is a lifecycle assessment indicator of ecosystem damage integrated over time, not a literal count of locally observed extinctions. QNBI depends on the task-specific quality evaluation. These results do not cover every effect of data centers, training, or agentic systems.
